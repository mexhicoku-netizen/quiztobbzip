How to format a quiz

The converter reads quizzes the way teachers normally write them. You do not need special codes. These are the layouts it understands.

Questions and options

Start each question with a number: 1. 1) Q1. Question 1: or Word’s own automatic numbering. Options start with a letter: a) A. (b) or Word’s automatic lettering. Several options on one line are fine.

1. What is the SI unit of force?
a) Joule
b) Newton
c) Watt
d) Pascal

2. Which is a vector? a) mass b) speed c) velocity d) time

Where the answers come from

For each question the first of these that is found wins:

  1. The key box or a key file on the converter page: B C A D, 1B 2C 3A, or a mark-scheme .docx/.txt.
  2. An answer-key section inside the quiz, under a heading such as Answer key, Answers, Mark scheme or Key: pairs like 1. B, 1-B, 1) b, or a table with a row of numbers above a row of letters.
  3. A line under the question: Answer: B, Ans: (b), Correct answer: C or Key: D.
  4. A marked option: *B) ..., B) ... *, B) ... (correct), B) ... ✓ or [x] B) ....
  5. Formatting in Word: the one option that is bold, highlighted, underlined or coloured differently from the others.
3. Which law gives F = G m1 m2 / r^2?
A. Hooke's law
B. Newton's third law
*C. Newton's law of gravitation
D. Kepler's second law

True / false and written answers

A question with the options True and False, or one that says “true or false” in the stem, becomes a true/false question. A question with no options becomes a written-answer (essay) question that you mark by hand. If your key has a model answer for it (6. Period squared is proportional to ...) it is attached for marking.

4. True or false: a satellite in circular orbit has constant speed.
Answer: True

5. Explain why astronauts seem weightless in orbit. (3 marks)

Points and feedback

Write (2 pt.), [3 marks] or (1 point) in a question to set its points (otherwise each question gets the default, 1). A line starting Feedback: or Explanation: under a question is shown to students after they answer.

Pictures and tables

A picture placed under a question is packed into the test and shown under that question. To share one picture or table between several questions, put a line above it such as Use the diagram below to answer Questions 3–4. Each figure is also saved as Q03.png and so on, in the figures folder of the download. Pictures in .emf or .wmf format often do not show in an LMS: paste them into Word as PNG.

Equations

Word equations, LaTeX in \( ... \) or $ ... $, and equation pictures with LaTeX alt text are turned into plain maths text that every LMS shows, for example F = G m₁m₂/r², v = √(GM/r) and T² = 4π²/(GM) a³. Tick Keep LaTeX as typed if your LMS renders LaTeX itself.

Plain text and Markdown

.txt and .md files follow the same rules. In Markdown, **bold** and ==highlight== count as formatting for the answer, and pipe tables are read as tables.

Check before you import

The results table shows every question, the answer found, how it was found, and any figure. Red rows have no answer. Skim it against your paper copy once; it takes a minute and catches almost everything.
